# Go-to-market — first 30 days

## Channel thesis

The buyer already searches for change-order templates and asks peers how to charge for added work. Launch material should teach the exact workflow first: describe the change, state the price and days, get a documented acknowledgment, then start. No claims that the app guarantees payment or legal protection.

## 30-day sequence

1. **Days 1–3:** Record three silent local demos: kitchen layout change, added outlet, material substitution. Publish nothing under this build’s guardrails; keep drafts ready.
2. **Days 4–10:** Draft five short educational posts and one printable “change conversation” checklist. Observe relevant contractor communities only; do not message anyone under the guardrail.
3. **Days 11–20:** When publication is authorized, test two messages: “price the extra before you do it” vs. “change-order PDF in two minutes.” Link to the free app, not a signup.
4. **Days 21–30:** Ask early users (only after external outreach is authorized) whether they printed/sent an order, what they used before, and whether they would pay $79. Iterate only on repeated friction.

## Metrics and kill criteria

Track: app opens, completed print actions, backup downloads, conversations, and paid licenses. After 100 qualified app opens, pause the paid offer if fewer than 10 people print/export or if zero people indicate willingness to pay; interview the workflow, not the branding.

## Draft hook

“The most expensive sentence on a jobsite is ‘while you’re here…’ when nobody writes down what it costs. Here is the one-page change-order workflow I use before the extra work starts.”
