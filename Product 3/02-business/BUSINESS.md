# Business design — Jobfold

## Customer and job

**Initial ICP:** owner-operators and 1–10 person residential remodeling/trade firms who already use texts, photos, and PDFs but do not want a full CRM. Their moment of value is on site, after an added request and before additional work begins.

**Job to be done:** “When a customer changes the work, help me turn the conversation into a clear price, schedule impact, and record without making the interaction adversarial.”

## Offer

- Free local demo: create and print a change order.
- Founding License: **$79 once** for one contractor; unlimited local change orders.
- Office Pack hypothesis: **$199 once** for five users, if a later sync-free distribution path proves useful.

The pricing is a hypothesis. The proof target is five paid licenses from conversations with the ICP—not impressions or email signups.

## Wedge and alternatives

Jobfold is not a replacement for HoneyBook, Bonsai, Buildertrend, a lawyer, or a signed construction contract. It is the fast, local “write the change down now” tool before a contractor needs any of those systems. The evidence dossier documents why broad subscription tools are the relevant alternative category.

## Unit economics

Static site + single HTML app means no required hosting, database, account, or per-document API cost in MVP. The main cost is founder time. At a $79 price, one sale validates payment and can cover a domain/hosting decision later; no domain purchase is authorized in this build.

## Risks and answers

| Risk | Mitigation / test |
|---|---|
| Contractors may prefer a template | Offer the app free; ask whether price + photo + backup flow is materially faster than their template. |
| Legal expectations | Repeatedly say “documented acknowledgment,” not “legally binding.” |
| Job records need sharing | Validate demand before adding any server or account. |
| Full platforms bundle the feature | Sell the low-friction, no-adoption wedge, not feature parity. |
