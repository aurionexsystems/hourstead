# Askstead — Go-to-Market Plan (launch month: July 2026)

*Nothing posted anywhere, per guardrail 2. This is the owner's playbook.*

## Positioning
For local business owners who want Google reviews without renting a $400/month platform, Askstead is a print-it-yourself review-ask kit — $49 once, compliant with Google's and the FTC's rules by construction, nothing to cancel because nothing renews.

## Who buys, and where they are
Unlike Hourstead's audience, main-street owners are **not** on Hacker News. They're in trade Facebook groups, local-business subreddits, YouTube how-to search, and — critically — they ask Google itself.

## Channels, in order

### 1. SEO on the money queries (weeks 1–4; the core engine)
The buying moment is a search. Pages to ship, each anchored to verified facts from the dossier:
- "How to get a Google review QR code (free, 10 minutes)" — captures the DIY intent, upsells the kit.
- "Podium alternatives for small business (2026 prices)" — the category's own comparison content proves the query pays; ours is the only one whose answer is "$49 once."
- "Is review gating illegal? Google's policy + the FTC rule, in plain English" — compliance content nobody else *wants* to rank for; enforcement news (first FTC letters Dec 2025) keeps it fresh.
- "Google review link: where to find yours" — pure utility, top of funnel.

### 2. Local-SEO agencies & consultants (weeks 2–6; the multiplier)
Freelancers who set up review funnels for many clients buy multiple kits and become the sales force. Offer the Agency Bundle: **$149 for 5 kit licenses** (vs $245 bought singly). Reach them where they already discuss Podium pricing: local-SEO newsletters, r/localseo-type communities, BrightLocal's audience (their survey is our shared vocabulary).

### 3. LinkedIn — the agency-channel amplifier (weeks 1–6, then standing)
Owner directive (2026-07-10): LinkedIn is a priority marketing channel. Honest fit assessment: main-street owners (dentists, plumbers, salons) are thin on LinkedIn — but **local-SEO consultants, agency owners, and SMB advisors (accountants, business coaches) are dense there**, and they're exactly channel 2's multiplier. So LinkedIn primarily sells the Agency Bundle and earns referrals, not single kits.
- **Motion:** founder posts from a personal profile, organic only (no ads during zero-budget phase), 2/week.
- **Post archetypes:** (a) the compliance wedge — "review gating is banned and the FTC started sending letters in December; here's what compliant asking looks like" (cites the fetched FTC/Google sources — advisors reshare compliance content because it makes *them* look diligent); (b) the unbundling story — "$399/month vs $49 once: what review platforms actually do" with the BBB evidence; (c) agency-facing — "set up a client's review funnel in 10 minutes" walkthrough + Agency Bundle ($149/5).
- **Direct outreach:** the GTM's "10 local-SEO practitioners" (D15–21) happens via LinkedIn DM — warm, disclosed, free-kit-first.
- **Measure:** bundle sales and advisor reshares; ignore vanity impressions.
- **Ready-to-post material:** full 7-day conversion campaign (posts, first comments, advisor DM scripts, harvest-week plan, revenue targets) in [../linkedin/7-day-campaign.md](../linkedin/7-day-campaign.md).

### 4. YouTube (weeks 3–8)
One honest 6-minute video: "Get more Google reviews with a $49 kit (no subscription)" — screen-record the generator, print the card, scan it live. How-to search traffic compounds like SEO.

### 5. Communities (opportunistic, disclosed)
r/smallbusiness, r/sweatystartup, trade groups (dental office managers, HVAC owners). Lead with the free kit and the compliance explainer, never with the pitch.

### 6. FTC/Google enforcement news cycle (standing play)
Every enforcement action or policy update is a moment when "is our review tool compliant?" gets asked. The compliance explainer page + a calm comment is the response. (Same mechanic as Hourstead's Bending Spoons cycle — own the trust position when the news does the fear.)

## Sales infrastructure (chosen, not purchased)
askstead.com (~$10/yr when bought) · static hosting free tier · Lemon Squeezy for the $49 license (keys use the implemented AS1 checksum format) · support = owner's email.

## 30-day calendar
- **D1–2:** domain, deploy site + generator, Lemon Squeezy product, license issuance tested.
- **D3–7:** ship SEO pages #1 and #4 (utility pages rank fastest), submit to Google Search Console.
- **D8–14:** Podium-alternatives page + compliance explainer; first community shares of the *free* kit; LinkedIn cadence starts (compliance-wedge post first — it's the most reshareable).
- **D15–21:** YouTube video; Agency Bundle ($149/5 kits) live; LinkedIn DM outreach to 10 local-SEO practitioners.
- **D22–30:** iterate on generator analytics-free signals (sales, support emails); second video or page based on what converted.

## Metrics & kill criteria
- 90-day bar (from BUSINESS.md): **≥30 licenses (~$1.5k) or ≥3 agency bundle buyers.** Below → keep free, stop pushing.
- Leading indicators: SEO impressions on money queries, free-kit generations (measurable only via page views — no telemetry in the app, by design).
- Qualitative win: one unsolicited "cancelled Podium, bought this" email.

## Risks
- **SEO takes months:** true; agencies (channel 2) and YouTube bridge the gap. Zero fixed costs mean patience is affordable.
- **Free-QR DIYers never convert:** acceptable — they were never the buyer; the kit sells packaging + compliance + confidence.
- **A platform ships a $49 tier:** would require them to publish a price. Their sales-call model suggests they won't (inference).
