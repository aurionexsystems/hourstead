We follow the rules. Loudly.

Google prohibits review gating — showing the review link only to customers you think are happy. The FTC's reviews rule (in force since October 2024, first enforcement letters sent December 2025) bans suppression and paid-for sentiment. Plenty of tools grew up doing exactly that.

Askstead is compliant by construction: every asset shows the public review path to everyone, private feedback is offered alongside it — never as a filter in front of it — and no script ever offers a discount for stars. This isn't just legal hygiene. Asking honestly is why the reviews you get are worth having.

Fair questions

Can't I just make a QR code for free?

Yes — and you'll get a naked QR square. The kit is the part after that: print-ready cards that look professional, a feedback page that follows Google's and the FTC's rules, and asks that are worded to convert. $49 buys not having to become a designer and a compliance reader this weekend.

Where does my data go?

Nowhere. The generator runs in your browser. We never see your business name, your link, or your customers. There's no account to create.

What do I need to start?

Your free Google review link (Business Profile → "Ask for reviews"), a browser, and a printer. The kit walks you through finding the link.

Does asking for reviews actually work?

The ask is the whole game: most customers who'd happily review you simply never think of it. A visible, effortless prompt at the moment of satisfaction is what the $400/month platforms are selling underneath everything else.

What if Askstead disappears?

Your printed cards keep working — they're paper pointing at Google's own link. Your counter page is a file you host. Like everything we make, the exit is built in.
