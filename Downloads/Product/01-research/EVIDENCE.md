## 3. What the playbook already is (we did not invent it)

Fetched: [NerdWallet, “Home Insurance Companies May Use Aerial Images to Drop Policies”](https://www.nerdwallet.com/insurance/homeowners/learn/aerial-imagery-homeowners-insurance)

Red flags they list (we copied these into Walk the Lot):

- Debris in the yard (Karl Susman: vermin / trip-and-fall)
- Trees hanging over the home (Susman: fire + storm)
- Trampolines or pools the insurer wasn’t told about (Bob Passmore, APCIA)
- Roof moss, broken shingles, other wear (Alaina Hixson)

What to do (Heller / Hixson / Passmore / Bach, same page):

1. Immediately ask why. If aerial images were used, ask to see them and **when they were taken**.
2. If outdated or inaccurate: contest. Bach: wrong house; skylights mistaken for roofing.
3. Hixson: *“If you can show documentation that something isn't what they think it is, an insurer will often work with you.”* Submit your own photos, a licensed roofer’s photos, a contractor receipt; request an in-person inspection.
4. If the photo is accurate: they may extend if you fix it. If you can’t, shop immediately. A nonrenewal on the record can make the next policy harder.
5. If you think it was unfair: file with the state department of insurance.
6. Heller: about two months before renewal, get quotes from other insurers.

State limits the same NerdWallet page reports (journalism summarizing regulators — **not** the statutes; we say so in the product):

- **Georgia:** images ≤ 1 year old; homeowner must get to see them; ≥ 60 days to fix.
- **Indiana:** if images are the only reason, insurer must say how to see copies; images ≤ 2 years; ≥ 60 days to fix.
- **Louisiana:** may not use images older than two years as the *sole* reason.
- **Maryland:** cannot use unclear or outdated images to justify cancel/nonrenew; must investigate further; department encourages sharing images and a chance to dispute or fix.

## 6. What we are *not* claiming

- That aerial imagery is illegal. NerdWallet/Passmore: usually legal; “another method of doing something insurers have always been doing.”
- That every nonrenewal is an imagery error. NAIC does not split causes. Market exits are real.
- That a packet guarantees renewal. Bennett’s reversal involved ABC7. HN user w10-1’s carrier relented after **remediation + photos** — which may mean the photos were accurate.
- Any willingness-to-pay number. **Inference:** a household staring at a $20k roof they do not believe they need will consider $49. Untested.
