“attempting to collect and report medical debt that I do not owe … even though the original medical provider confirmed that my balance is {$0.00}.” CFPB Consumer Complaint Database, complaint received 2026-07-29, Ability Recovery Services, LLC — public API extract, redactions theirs.

In February 2025, NBC Connecticut named Lisa Horan of Somers: she paid a $2,206 ER invoice; the check was cashed; a collector texted her anyway.

The CFPB’s own (archived) newsroom line is the category:

“Many Americans feel forced to pay medical bills that they have already paid or never owed to begin with.” Rohit Chopra, CFPB — consumerfinance.gov newsroom, medical billing bulletin.

What changed in 2025, and did not un-change

A CFPB rule that would have pulled medical debt off credit reports was vacated on 11 July 2025 (Eastern District of Texas; the Bureau’s own rule page says so). Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion still say medical collections under $500 stay off U.S. files — a 2023 policy, not a law. Collectors still write. The thirty-day validation window in 15 U.S.C. § 1692g still exists.

Who this is for

You have a statement, an EOB that does not match it, or a collector text. You can type. You will not upload your member ID to a startup so their model can “handle it.”

If your income may qualify at a nonprofit hospital, start with Dollar For (free; hospital charity care only; they say so). If you want a human to call, and the bill is unpaid and not in collections, Goodbill’s published consumer fee is 20% of savings, capped at $1,000. If the bill is already paid, or already at a collector, those doors are often closed. That is the gap.
