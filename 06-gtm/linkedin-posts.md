## Post 2 — The evidence story (archetype b) — week 1

Your time-tracking tool now charges you per invoice you send.

Not a hypothetical. Harvest's pricing page, today: paid plans are $9–14/seat/month, and "additional invoices, projects, clients, and tasks are billed based on what you use."

The company that owns Harvest also owns Evernote (free tier cut to 50 notes), WeTransfer (cut to 10 transfers/month), and Meetup. It IPO'd this month at $25B+. Public-market growth pressure meets a documented price-hike playbook.

## Post 3 — Practical (archetype c) — week 2

The most important file in a freelance business isn't the portfolio. It's the ledger — every hour, every client, every invoice.

Most of us keep it inside a subscription. Which means we don't keep it at all; we visit it.

Three questions worth answering this week, whatever tool you use:
1. Can you export everything, today, without emailing support?
2. Is the export in a format you could open in ten years?
3. If the vendor tripled prices at renewal, what would leaving cost you?

Hourstead's answer is boring on purpose: the whole business is one JSON file on your disk. But whatever you use — go check your exit door. It's cheaper to find a locked door before you need it.
